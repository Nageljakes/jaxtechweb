You don't need to read this page to work with Jaxtech — most people never do. But if you want to know what you're actually buying, and why it behaves differently to anything off a shelf, here it is without the marketing.

The controller

Every Jaxtech home installation is built around a small computer that lives on your property — currently an Ubuntu box called tiny — talking to Home Assistant, not an app talking to someone else's data centre. It's the difference between a home that depends on the internet and one that merely uses it.

The controller sips power behind your inverter or a small UPS and holds the automations: bedroom occupancy + light levels, camera face-gating, voice via Assist. When connectivity returns after an outage, queued notifications sync. It never waits for permission to do its job.

Perception

mmWave occupancy, lux, cameras, and device states read continuously — locally.

Reasoning

Rules plus local models decide what a situation means and what happens next.

Action

Lights, scenes, notifications and camera alerts driven through Home Assistant.

Memory

History and preferences stay on-site — manual overrides always win for 2 hours.

Why local AI matters

Cloud platforms send camera footage and household telemetry elsewhere for processing. That's convenient for them and expensive for you — in privacy, bandwidth, and reliability when either end fails.

On my own network today: Frigate does person/face/plate detection on the AI server GPU, the bedroom service fuses occupancy + lux on tiny, and the model fleet (LM Studio on an RTX 3060, role-routed) answers code, diary and chat jobs over the LAN. Nothing about family movements needs to leave the property to be intelligent.

Being straight about the exception: big reasoning models are heavy. The Tiny chat on this site runs on hosted cloud inference — your message goes to an AI provider for a reply, and I read it if you enquire. A custom agent for you can run on cloud, on your own hardware, or both. We decide that first, based on sensitivity.

Where cloud genuinely adds value — forecasts, remote access, message delivery — it's an input, never a single point of failure.

A worked example

What "one coordinated system" actually looks like — taken from services running today:

22:00

Lights-out. The bedroom service force-offs the light on schedule — unless you overrode it manually in the last 2 hours, in which case it leaves you alone.

Night

mmWave occupancy + lux gate the light: dark + breathing present → on at 2000K/10%. No motion dancing. Auto-off 60s after the room clears.

Anytime

A person crosses the yard. Frigate waits 20s for recognition to settle — known face stays quiet, unknown face sends one Telegram photo with snapshot. No motion-spam.

04:00

Weekday wake fade: 20 steps from 2000K/5% to 4300K/100% over 25 minutes, only when you're home. Weekends shift to 06:00.

Day

Ask "hey Jarvis, turn off bedroom auto" and Assist obeys. Dashboard + voice + Telegram stay in sync because one service owns the state.

The engineering behind it

Jaxtech isn't a reseller putting someone else's box on your wall. The bedroom service, the face-gate, the model router and the dealership tooling are built here — by one person, which is why scope is always agreed honestly upfront.

Local model fleet

LM Studio on an RTX 3060, role-routed (code, crm, chat), single-GPU-resident with 5-min eviction. Smoke-tested, metered, LAN + Tailscale.

Systems & software

Python services under systemd, unit-tested (49 + 33 tests), version-controlled, with live logs — updates don't break your home.

Device & protocol

Tuya Local, MQTT, ONVIF/RTSP, Frigate + Home Assistant — cameras, lights, presence and voice behaving as one product.

Vision on the edge

Frigate + TensorRT YOLO, face recognition + plates, person/dog tracking. Detection stays on your network.

Dealership ops

Prospect diary management across today / overdue / completed lists — single verified moves, bulk day shifts with preview + undo, week equalization. Dual-logged records, WhatsApp companion indexing, sub-10 ms stock search. Built from real dealership shifts.

Automation design

Turning "the light annoys me" into a rule that works every day — manual always wins, quiet by default.

Ownership and honesty

The hardware is yours. The system runs whether or not you keep paying us. Automations are documented in plain language so you can see exactly what your house does and why, and anything consequential — gates, locks, spending — waits for a human decision by design, not as a setting you have to find.

We'd rather install a smaller system that you trust completely than a bigger one you quietly stop using.
