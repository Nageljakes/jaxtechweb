The controller

Every Jaxtech installation is built around a dedicated piece of hardware that lives on your property — not an app talking to someone else's data centre. It's the difference between a home that depends on the internet and one that merely uses it.

The controller draws very little power, sits behind your inverter or a small UPS, and holds the entire state of your home: every device, every rule, every event. When connectivity returns after an outage, it syncs. It never waits for permission to do its job.

Perception

Sensors, cameras, meters and device states continuously read and interpreted.

Reasoning

A local AI engine decides what a situation means and what should happen next.

Action

Lights, geysers, pumps, blinds, gates and notifications driven through one control layer.

Memory

History, routines and preferences stored privately on-site — the house learns your household.

Why local AI matters

Cloud smart-home platforms send your camera footage and household telemetry to a third party for processing. That's convenient for them and expensive for you — in privacy, in bandwidth, and in reliability the moment either end of the connection fails.

Jaxtech runs the models that matter on the box in your house. Person and vehicle recognition, presence detection, consumption analysis and the natural-language layer you chat to all execute locally. Nothing about your family's movements needs to leave the property for the system to be intelligent.

Where cloud services genuinely add value — weather forecasts, remote access when you're away, message delivery — we use them. As inputs, never as a dependency.

A worked example

What "one coordinated system" actually looks like on an ordinary Tuesday evening:

17:42

Grid drops. The controller detects the transfer to inverter and immediately holds the geyser and pool pump — protecting your battery reserve without you touching anything.

17:43

Fibre router loses power. Cameras keep recording and analysing locally; the outside lights still respond to presence because none of that logic lives in the cloud.

18:10

A vehicle stops at the gate. On-site recognition identifies it as unknown, so you get one clear message with a snapshot — not a generic motion alert.

21:30

Power returns. Queued notifications sync, the geyser reschedules to heat in the cheapest window before your morning shower, and irrigation checks tomorrow's forecast before committing.

06:00

Your morning briefing arrives on WhatsApp: what happened overnight, what the outage cost you, and what the house is planning today.

Systems & software engineering

Security engineering

Data & reporting

Automation & workflow design

The discipline of turning "the geyser annoys me" into a reliable rule that works every day without supervision.
